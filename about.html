About

I'm a process engineer at a refinery. This site is the stuff I wish someone had written down before my first technical interview.

Why this exists#

There's a gap between what chemical engineering students learn in school and what industry actually expects them to know. I found it the hard way, in an interview, when a senior principal engineer asked me where the deadhead point was on a pump curve and I had nothing. Everyone on that plant would call it a basic question. Four years and a 4.0 hadn't put it in front of me once.

So this is a free resource that tries to close that gap. Everything here is written first person from direct plant experience, not assembled out of textbooks. That's the only reason it's worth reading, and it's also the limit on how fast it can grow.

What's in scope#

Oil, gas, and refining, deliberately. I'd rather cover a narrow area honestly than a broad one badly. If you're going into pharma or specialty chemicals, some of this transfers and some of it won't, and I'd rather you know which is which.

Most of the topic list isn't written yet. It's all listed anyway, because a site that hides its gaps wastes your time.

On accuracy#

My depth isn't the same across every topic. Pumps and tanks I live with; compressors and fired heaters I'll need to research properly and have reviewed by someone who knows them before I publish. Each page tracks that internally, and pages where I'm on thinner ice say so out loud.

None of this is written by a language model. That's the whole point — a page a model could have produced has no reason to exist, and you can already get that anywhere.

If something here is wrong, I want to know. Corrections are the most useful thing you can send me, and I'd rather be embarrassed once than leave bad information in front of someone walking into an interview.

Getting in touch#

Corrections, questions you got asked in a technical interview, or topics you wish were covered: open an issue on GitHub.

Interview questions are especially welcome. The "questions people actually get asked" section on each page is only as good as the supply of real questions, and mine is one person's sample.

Written by one process engineer at a refinery. Corrections welcome.
